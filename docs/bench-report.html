Archetype Bench

Archetype bench — 136 heroes at 10★ Lv 100, ungeared, 7v7 within archetype, 25 seeded sims each, 60s window, 2%/s attrition

What this measures

Every hero is dropped into the same fight in place of one ally, against and alongside a fixed cast drawn from its own archetype. The only thing that changes between runs is the hero under test — which is what makes the comparison fair rather than realistic. Nobody carries gear, so the numbers are the kit and the statline and nothing else.

The cast is fixed

Six allies and seven opponents, the median-power members of the same bucket. Both formations are full: a half-empty board silently undervalues anything that hits a row.

Judged on one axis

A back-row healer and a front-row bruiser have no business on the same leaderboard, so each bucket is ranked on the thing that bucket exists for — damage, worth or effective HP.

Everything is a rate

Most mirrors run the clock out with no winner. That is fine and expected: every column is per-second, so a fight that never resolves still measures throughput.

Seeded

The same hero replays the same fight every time, so a difference between two heroes is a difference in the heroes and not in the dice.

Value — the whole roster on one scale

Six axes, levelled, added up, and pinned to an ordinary 3★

Everything above judges a hero against its own bucket, which is right for balancing a role and useless for “is this 5★ actually better than that 3★”. Value answers that instead. Every hero is run against one shared cast — not its bucket's — on its own hex, and six things are measured: offence, durability, healing, buffing, debuffing and tempo (action bar taken off enemies and handed to allies).

Each figure is divided by the roster mean of that figure, so an axis only a fifth of the roster can post at all is worth five times as much to the fifth that can — a healer is not punished for dealing no damage. Each is then square rooted, because an axis saturates: the fourth healer in a squad of seven is worth less than the first. Compression does not bite every axis equally, so each is rescaled to carry exactly one sixth of the table. The sum is divided through by the 3★ cohort's average, which is what 1.00 means: an ordinary 3★'s worth. Off the curve: 1★ -0.31, 2★ +0.15.

The rarity curve

What a star is worth, measured

The goal: a 5★ around 1.30× its bucket median, a 3★ on it, and the cheap shelves below. Each row is the median of every hero on that shelf, across all six buckets.

dearest shelf now benches 2.03× the cheapest read straight off the CSV, not transcribed

That last column used to be the finding, and it ran the wrong way: a 1★ benched at 3.6× the power of a 5★. Two rules were contradicting each other. Base budgets were equal for everybody, and rarity was supposed to live only in starting stars, level cap and summon odds — but the star multiplier in js/progression.js compounded 1.25× per star gained above a hero's base rarity, so a 1★ climbing to 10★ took nine steps and a 5★ took five. At the shared ceiling that was 2.44× in favour of the cheap hero, for 45 duplicates against 35. Equal base stats times an inverted multiplier is an inverted roster.

Both halves moved. The star multiplier now counts stars alone, so a 10★ is a 10★ whatever shelf it came from, and the curve is anchored at three stars — a 3★ hero's numbers are unchanged at every star and every level, which matters because every enemy the game seats is a 3★, so the campaign is still tuned against the same yardstick it was built on. Rarity is now priced in exactly one line, SHELF in js/data/balance.js: a flat multiplier on the finished stats, every step a multiple of 5%.

Lift — what the mirror cannot see

The same party twice against a fixed wave, once with the hero and once without

Lift bench — 136 heroes, 15 paired fights each, cap 1600 ticks, vs six Barrington at Lv 40 5★

A mirror gives both sides the same party buff, so it cancels and every pure buffer reads at or below nothing above. This bench is asymmetric instead: paired on the seed, the only difference is whether the hero is on the board, and the reading is ticks saved on the clear. Positive is faster. A row within one standard error of zero is drawn flat, because that is not a reading. Every hero cleared the wave in all 25 paired runs, so no reading here is censored.

Read this as clear speed, not worth. The party wins this wave comfortably and nobody dies in it, so healing and mitigation buy almost nothing here and a hero whose value is survival will read low by construction. It is the companion to the mirror, not its verdict.
